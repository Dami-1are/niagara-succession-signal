## 1. Canadian Business Counts

**Researched by:** Theophilus & Emmanuella

### Canadian Business Counts, with employees, census metropolitan areas and census subdivisions, June 2026

- **Organisation:** Statistics Canada
- **Table:** 33-10-1176-01
- **Release date:** August 14, 2026
- **Frequency:** Semi-annual
- **Geography:** Census metropolitan areas and census subdivisions
- **Information available:** Business location counts by employment-size range and North American Industry Classification System (NAICS)
- **Use in our project:** Helps us understand the number, size, industry and location of employer businesses at a more local geographic level.
- **Official source:** https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/catalogue/3310117601

### Canadian Business Counts, with employees, June 2026

- **Organisation:** Statistics Canada
- **Table:** 33-10-1174-01
- **Release date:** August 14, 2026
- **Frequency:** Semi-annual
- **Geography:** Canada, provinces and territories
- **Information available:** Business location counts by employment-size range and NAICS
- **Use in our project:** Provides Ontario and Canada business-count benchmarks.
- **Official source:** https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/catalogue/3310117401

### Canadian Business Counts, without employees, June 2026

- **Organisation:** Statistics Canada
- **Table:** 33-10-1175-01
- **Release date:** August 14, 2026
- **Frequency:** Semi-annual
- **Geography:** Canada, provinces and territories
- **Information available:** Business location counts without employees by NAICS
- **Use in our project:** Helps us understand businesses without employees at the provincial and national level.
- **Official source:** https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/catalogue/3310117501

### Important Limitation

Canadian Business Counts can tell us how many businesses exist, their size, industry and geography, but it does not directly tell us whether a business owner is retiring or whether a business has a succession plan.

## 3. Census and Demographics

**Researched by:** Precious & Ruth

### Age Data

- **Organisation:** Statistics Canada
- **Table:** 98-10-0022-01
- **Years:** 2016 and 2021
- **Geography:** Census divisions and census subdivisions
- **Information available:** Age in single years, average age and median age
- **Use in our project:** Helps us understand the age structure of Niagara Region and its municipalities.
- **Official source:** https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=9810002201

### Class of Worker / Self-Employment

- **Organisation:** Statistics Canada
- **Table:** 98-10-0592-01
- **Data source:** 2021 Census, 25% sample data
- **Information available:** Class of worker by industry, labour-force status, age and gender
- **Use in our project:** Helps us understand self-employment patterns by age and industry.
- **Official source:** https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=9810059201

### Important Limitation

Self-employed people should not automatically be treated as business owners.

The Census information can help us understand possible demographic patterns, but it does not directly identify the age of individual Niagara business owners or whether they have succession plans.

The 2021 Census labour information also reflects conditions during the COVID-19 period.

## 4. Niagara Employment Inventory and Open Data

**Researched by:** Stephen & Mano

### Consolidated NEI Opendata

- **Organisation:** Niagara Region
- **Dataset:** Consolidated NEI Opendata
- **Geography:** Niagara Region
- **Information available:** Niagara business and employment information, including location/municipality, industry/NAICS information, business opening year and employee-size information.
- **Use in our project:** This is one of our strongest local sources for understanding Niagara's business structure, sectors, municipalities and employment size.
- **Available formats:** Multiple formats are available through Niagara Open Data.
- **Official source:** https://niagaraopendata.ca/dataset/consolidated-nei-opendata

### 2026 Niagara Employment Inventory

- **Organisation:** Niagara Economic Development
- **Important finding:** The 2026 Employment Inventory survey introduced questions relating to succession planning.
- **Use in our project:** These questions may provide more direct information about business succession in Niagara if the resulting data can be accessed.
- **Current status:** We have not yet confirmed that the succession-specific responses are publicly available.
- **Official source:** https://niagaracanada.com/more-than-13000-businesses-to-be-visited-employment-inventory-building-snapshot-of-niagaras-economy/

### Important Limitation

The publicly accessible NEI data we reviewed should not be described as containing owner age, retirement intention, identified successor or succession-plan status unless those fields are confirmed in the downloaded data.

Access to the 2026 succession-specific information requires further investigation.

## 5. Business Succession Research

**Researched by:** Oluwatosin & OHONBA

### Succession Tsunami: Preparing for a Decade of Small Business Transitions in Canada

- **Organisation:** Canadian Federation of Independent Business (CFIB)
- **Published:** January 10, 2023
- **Survey period:** June to August 2022
- **Coverage:** Canada
- **Information available:** Business-owner exit intentions, expected exit timelines, succession planning and barriers to succession.
- **Use in our project:** Provides direct research evidence about succession issues affecting Canadian small and medium-sized businesses.
- **Official source:** https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/succession-tsunami-preparing-for-a-decade-of-small-business-transitions

### Important Limitation

The CFIB study is national research.

Its percentages should not automatically be presented as if they describe Niagara businesses specifically.

It will be used as supporting research and a national benchmark unless we later establish a valid method for applying any part of it locally.

# Week 2 Main Data Gap

Our Week 2 research identified an important gap.

We found strong Niagara-specific information about:

- Businesses
- Municipalities
- Industries
- Employment
- Business size
- Local economic conditions

We also found broader Canadian information about:

- Business-owner retirement
- Exit intentions
- Succession planning
- Business entry and exit
- Barriers to succession

However, we have not yet confirmed a publicly available Niagara-specific dataset containing direct information such as:

- Business-owner age
- Retirement intention
- Intended exit timeline
- Identified successor
- Succession-plan status

This does not mean that the information does not exist.

The 2026 Niagara Employment Inventory included succession-planning questions, so access to the resulting information will be investigated further.

---

# Week 2 Research Decision

Based on our research, we will use different sources for different purposes rather than expecting one dataset to answer the entire research question.

### Local Business Structure

The Niagara Employment Inventory and Canadian Business Counts will help us understand Niagara businesses by location, sector and size.

### Business Entry and Exit

Statistics Canada Business Dynamics data will help us understand historical business entry and exit patterns.

### Demographic Context

Statistics Canada Census data will help us understand Niagara's age structure and self-employment patterns.

### Succession Context

CFIB succession research will provide broader Canadian evidence about business-owner retirement, exit intentions, succession planning and barriers.

### Economic Context

Niagara Economic Development data will support our understanding of employment, sectors and the local economy.

# Week 3 — Data Collection and Audit

The strongest sources identified during Week 2 will move into formal data collection and audit in Week 3.

Before combining datasets or using them for analysis, we will check:

- File format
- Number of rows and columns
- Column names
- Data types
- Years covered
- Geography
- Industry classification
- Business-size definitions
- Missing values
- Duplicate records
- Licensing and access
- Data limitations
- Comparability between sources

We will preserve the original raw datasets before making changes.

We will not treat a business closure as proof of succession failure.

We will not treat self-employment as direct proof of business ownership.

We will not apply national succession percentages directly to Niagara without a valid method.

We will not build a succession-risk model until the required datasets have been properly collected, audited and understood.

## Time-Coverage Rule

We will not assume that data from different years can be directly compared.

Before combining or comparing sources in later stages, we will check:

- the exact reference year or period;
- whether definitions changed between years;
- whether NAICS classifications changed;
- whether geography changed;
- whether business-size definitions changed; and
- whether unusual periods such as COVID-19 may affect interpretation.

Historical coverage does not automatically mean that all years form one directly comparable time series.
