## 4. Missing and suppressed values

Different sources use different conventions, and they must not be treated as the same thing. A suppressed value does not mean zero.

| Dataset | Convention (meaning from source metadata or approved cleaning report) |
|---|---|
| Canadian Business Counts | `STATUS`, `SYMBOL` and `TERMINATED` are blank in all 1,047,957 raw rows, so the raw file contains no suppression or quality symbols (Confirmed: CBC audit). `VALUE` has no zeros, no negatives and no blanks; the smallest value is 1. An absent combination therefore cannot be distinguished from a true zero or an unpublished value. Treatment during cleaning (cleaning report): the empty `STATUS`, `SYMBOL` and `TERMINATED` columns were removed from the cleaned file and remain in the raw file; no absent combination was filled with zero; the cleaned analytical fields have 0 missing values |
| Business Dynamics by Industry | Meanings from the cleaning report: `x` = suppressed to meet confidentiality requirements; `..` = not available for a specific reference period; blank status = an observed numeric `VALUE`. Ontario cleaned output: 2,448 blank, 36 `..` (each with a missing `VALUE`), 0 `x`. Whole-table counts per code are not reported. `SYMBOL` and `TERMINATED` were empty and were removed from the cleaned output. Missing values were not replaced with zero and not imputed; a `status_interpretation` column was added (audit, Section 3.6; cleaning report, Sections 2.3 and 2.7) |
| Business Dynamics by Firm Size | `x` = suppressed to meet the confidentiality requirements of the Statistics Act. Raw table: 4,179 missing `VALUE` entries, all `x` (audit, Section 4.6). Ontario cleaned output: 632 observed values and 196 `x` rows, left missing and not converted to zero, imputed or estimated; a `status_interpretation` column was added. `SYMBOL` and `TERMINATED` were removed from the cleaned output (cleaning report, Sections 3.3 and 3.6) |

## 5. Aggregate and overlapping categories

These can cause double counting if values are summed. The labels indicate overlap; whether any given sum double counts has not been tested in this document.

| Dataset | Aggregate or overlapping categories |
|---|---|
| Canadian Business Counts | "Total, with employees" overlaps the eight employment-size bands. NAICS includes aggregate values (Total, all industries [1]; Unclassified [2]; Sub-total, classified [3]) alongside specific industries, and parent and child NAICS levels overlap (22 industry names appear under more than one code). Census metropolitan area rows overlap the census subdivision rows. There is no Niagara Region total (Confirmed: CBC audit). The cleaned file adds flag columns to filter on before summing: `naics_category_type`, `employment_size_type` and `naics_level_name`. CMA rows are in a separate file (`geo_level` = `cma_reference_only`) and must not be added to municipality totals (cleaning report) |
| NEI | An `Aggregate` size category (22 rows, 2025 only) whose meaning is not established; short size labels (`Small`, `Micro`, `Medium`, `Large`) appear only in 2025 alongside the detailed labels such as `Small (5-99 Employees)`; `NEI_ID` repeats across years, so rows from different years may describe the same business (NEI audit, Sections 7 and 11; to confirm) |
| Business Dynamics by Industry | "Private sector" total alongside 17 other categories (16 NAICS sectors and "Unclassified businesses"); the private-sector total may not equal the sum of the industries, and counts are not necessarily additive across years (audit, Sections 3.5 and 3.7) |
| Business Dynamics by Firm Size | Nine firm-size categories that are not mutually exclusive: "Private sector" (total); "50 employees or more" overlaps "50 to less than 100 employees" and "100 employees or more"; "100 employees or more" overlaps "100 to less than 500 employees" and "500 employees or more". They must not be summed (audit, Section 4.5). The cleaning did not sum them and did not recode them as Micro, Small, Medium or Large; the source classification, which is based on Average Labour Units, was preserved (cleaning report, Section 3.5) |
| Census age | The 128 age labels mix single years, bands and statistics (average, median) across nested levels, so labels overlap; one label has a trailing space (`"64 "`). Canada, province, census division and census subdivision rows overlap geographically, and CSD totals do not exactly equal the Canada total (census audit, Part A, Section 13). The cleaning did not sum age categories. Using only `Total - Age`, the 12 municipalities equal the Niagara census division: 447,890 (2016) and 477,940 (2021) (cleaning report) |
| Census class of worker | Rows span several NAICS hierarchy levels. The "All classes of workers" and "Total - Class of worker" measures overlap the individual classes. Self-employed [5] also includes unpaid family workers while [6] does not (metadata notes 19 and 20, per census audit, Part B, Section 13). The cleaning added `class_worker_label` to tell the two apart and kept both without combining them. Age groups overlap (for example 25 to 64 includes 25 to 34) and industries are hierarchical (for example Farms inside Agriculture, forestry, fishing and hunting); neither may be summed without care (cleaning report, Sections 4.4, 6.2, 6.3) |
| Succession tables | Employment-size bands 1 to 4, 5 to 19, 20 to 99 and 100 or more, NAICS industry, business type, business age, urban/rural and ownership breakdowns, and answer categories that include nested or "unknown" groupings (for example timelines inside the "yes, intends to sell or transfer" answer). The audit does not describe which rows are totals or overlaps, so confirm from the table metadata (succession audit, Sections 3 to 6) |
| CFIB evidence | Exit-method percentages (49%, 24%, 23% and 21%) are retained as separate observations; they are not mutually exclusive and add to more than 100%. The timeline-specific plan percentages apply only to owners exiting within that timeline, which the extract keeps in a population field (succession audit, Section 7; evidence notes, Section 3) |
| Other datasets | Total industries; census division totals alongside municipalities; broad NAICS sectors alongside detailed industries; age totals alongside individual ages; overlapping firm-size categories. Per-dataset detail: To confirm from each audit |

## 6. NAICS

| Dataset | NAICS version and format |
|---|---|
| Canadian Business Counts | NAICS 2022 (Confirmed: metadata footnote 5). The code is embedded in the label text (for example `Accommodation and food services [72]`). Code depth is 1 to 4 digits; 445 unique values in the raw file. Cleaned file: `naics_label` and `naics_code` (text), parent and child codes kept, 403 distinct codes for the 12 municipalities (cleaning report) |
| Business Dynamics by Industry | Four NAICS versions across the series: 1997 NAICS (2001), 2002 NAICS (2002 to 2006), 2007 NAICS (2007 to 2011) and 2012 NAICS (2012 onward). 18 categories at sector level with the code in brackets (for example [44-45]); "Private sector" and "Unclassified businesses" carry no code (audit, Section 3.5). The cleaned output adds `industry_label`, `naics_code` (including ranges such as 31-33, 44-45 and 48-49) and `naics_version`. Versions are documented and not harmonised; strict cross-year comparison would need a Statistics Canada concordance (cleaning report, Sections 2.5 and 2.6). The report dates 2012 NAICS as 2012 to 2023 |
| Business Dynamics by Firm Size | No industry or NAICS dimension (audit, Section 4) |
| NEI | Primary NAICS is populated in all 98,065 rows as six-digit codes (for example 312120, 541212, 722511); Secondary NAICS is populated in 78,661 rows; Primary Sector and Industry are populated in all rows. The NAICS version or classification year is not stated in the audit (To confirm) |
| Census class of worker | NAICS 2017 (field `Industry - Groups - NAICS 2017 (428A)`), with several hierarchy levels in the rows; the level represented is not established. The cleaned file keeps the source coordinate and adds `industry_id` (400 distinct identifiers) extracted from it. The datasets do not necessarily share the same NAICS version, hierarchy level, formatting or granularity. A field called NAICS in two datasets does not make them automatically joinable. Harmonisation is not done in this document.

## 7. Proxy variables

| Variable | What it provides | What it is NOT |
|---|---|---|
| Population age | Demographic context | Confirmed business-owner age |
| Self-employment | Labour-force and business context | Automatically equivalent to business ownership or succession risk |
| Business exit (Business Dynamics) | Information about business exits | An explanation of why the business exited, or evidence of a succession-related closure |
| Ontario succession percentages | Contextual evidence about the environment | Individual Niagara business probabilities |
| CFIB exit-intention and barrier percentages | National survey evidence about exit plans and obstacles | A probability of succession failure, business closure or inability to transfer, or a Niagara or Ontario measurement. The 54% barrier figure is a reported obstacle (evidence notes, Sections 3 and 4) |

## 8. Direct, proxy and contextual evidence

| Class | Meaning | Datasets or variables |
|---|---|---|
| Direct / business-level | Observed directly for a Niagara business | NEI business-level fields (location, sector and industry, NAICS, employee-size category, opening-year field, status category). Business-level evidence about the business itself, but with no succession variables (NEI audit, Section 9) |
| Proxy | May help approximate a concept without measuring it | Population age; self-employment; business exits |
| Contextual | Broader Ontario or Canadian evidence about the environment around Niagara businesses | Statistics Canada succession tables; CFIB evidence; Canadian Business Counts (municipal) and Business Dynamics (provincial/territorial, so Ontario-level context only) as context for business structure (classification to confirm with the team lead) |

Nothing is classified as direct evidence unless its source supports it.

The succession audit calls Table 33-10-1196-01 "direct evidence of succession intention". Here the four Statistics Canada succession tables and the CFIB report are classed as contextual, because their values are aggregate Canada or Ontario percentages and not observations of Niagara businesses (issue #22, Steps 2 and 14; see Section 10, item 26).

## 9. What the data cannot tell us

For Niagara businesses, the project's data does not directly tell us:

- the owner's age;
- retirement intention;
- whether a succession plan exists;
- whether a successor has been identified;
- why a business closed;
- whether a closure was caused by succession;
- whether a particular business will close.

For Canadian Business Counts specifically, the downloaded file has no columns for any of these (Confirmed: CBC audit, Section 10). It also covers employer businesses only, so businesses without employees are not represented.

For the NEI, the 20 downloaded columns contain none of these either. `Permanently Closed or Vacant` (17,809 rows) is a status indicator only: no closure date, closure reason or vacancy reason is present (Confirmed: NEI audit, Sections 8 and 9). This does not establish that the 2026 NEI survey collected no succession information.

For Business Dynamics, neither table contains any of these variables. An exit is defined by employee presence across consecutive years, so it is not evidence of succession, retirement or ownership transfer (Confirmed: audit, Sections 3.4, 3.8, 4.4 and 4.7).

For the census tables, neither contains owner age, retirement intention, succession plan, successor, ownership transfer or closure reason (census audit, Section 10 of each part). Population age is not owner age, and self-employment does not establish business ownership.

The succession tables and the CFIB report give succession intentions, plans and barriers only as aggregate percentages for Canada or Ontario. They do not identify individual Niagara owners, plans, successors or closures, and the percentages must not be treated as probabilities for individual Niagara businesses (succession audit, Sections 8 and 9).
